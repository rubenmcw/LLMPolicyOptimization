Original Project Direction

Our initial goal was to implement and extend the method from Bosio et al. (2025), which automatically synthesizes robot controllers as short, interpretable Python programs generated by a large language model (LLM). For each generated controller program, the paper tunes numerical constants such as gains and thresholds using gradient-free optimization to maximize closed-loop performance.

We originally planned to focus on an end-to-end replication in a robot simulator, including an evaluation harness, code sandboxing, program parameterization, and an iterative database-driven search loop. As the semester progressed, our work shifted from a direct replication of the Bosio pipeline toward applying FunSearch-inspired policy exploration to the pendubot, building a more realistic custom simulation model, and developing a hardware-informed three-phase swing-up controller.

Semester Project Summary

From LLM-Generated Policies to a Working Pendubot Swing-Up Controller

Over the semester, our team explored whether LLM-guided program search could help synthesize interpretable controllers for a pendubot swing-up task. We began by testing FunSearch-style policies in simulation, then moved toward a custom pendubot model and a hardware-informed three-phase controller. Our final result was a working pendubot swing-up that starts from the downward position and reaches the upright equilibrium at the top.

Final Result Successful pendubot swing-up to the upright equilibrium

The completed controller combines simulation-driven exploration, hardware testing, and classical control structure to move the physical pendubot from the stable downward equilibrium to the upright equilibrium at the top.

01

LLM / FunSearch Policy Exploration

We first used FunSearch-style generated policies in DeepMind Control's prebuilt double-pendulum environment. The generated policies were evaluated visually through simulation videos, then hand-pruned when parts of the policy were physically nonsensical or unhelpful.

02

Simulation Lessons

Early generated policies produced interesting behaviors, but they struggled when the system started from rest near the downward equilibrium. To investigate whether those early policies could swing up from a more favorable initial configuration, the elbow was initialized near pi in some simulation tests.

03

Custom Pendubot Model

After discovering a large gap between the prebuilt simulation and the physical pendubot, we pivoted to a custom DeepMind Control model using a custom MuJoCo XML with physics parameters chosen to better match the real pendubot hardware.

04

Hardware-Informed Controller

The final controller was no longer a purely generated policy. Instead, it became a physically motivated three-phase swing-up strategy, with the first rock-up phase loosely inspired by behavior found during FunSearch exploration.

Final Three-Phase Control Strategy

The final controller uses different behavior depending on where the pendubot is in the swing-up process. This structure helped bridge the gap between interesting simulated behavior and a controller that could work on the actual hardware.

Phase 1: Rock-Up

The rock-up phase gets the pendubot out of the stable downward equilibrium. It is phase-dependent and behaves like a bang-bang controller: one side of the equilibrium receives positive torque, while the opposite side receives negative torque.

Phase 2: Pump-Up

Outside approximately 0.92 radians, or about 52°, the controller pumps energy into the system using the negative shoulder angular velocity. A tanh term smooths the torque instead of immediately saturating it.

Phase 3: LQR Catch

Near the top, the controller switches to a prebuilt LQR controller to catch and stabilize the pendubot at the upright equilibrium. This final catch phase uses both the shoulder and elbow actuators.

Controller Results

Key Takeaways

  • LLM-generated policies were useful for exploration, but not sufficient by themselves for reliable hardware transfer.
  • The initial DeepMind Control double-pendulum simulation had too much of a sim-to-real gap to transfer directly to the physical pendubot.
  • The first FunSearch-generated control policies produced interesting simulated behavior, but they were not reliable enough to serve as the final hardware controller.
  • The gap between the prebuilt simulation and the real pendubot motivated a custom MuJoCo XML model with more realistic physical parameters.
  • Early generated policies struggled from rest near the downward equilibrium, motivating a rock-up phase designed to move the system out of that stable starting position.
  • Hardware testing revealed that increasing torque alone could not break through the swing-up ceiling, motivating the smoother pump-up phase.
  • The final working solution combined ideas from FunSearch exploration with a structured controller: rock-up, pump-up, and LQR catch.
